# Changelog

Every version of SlotForge Timing, newest first. The README has a section **What to try (x.y.z)** for each version, with what changed, how to try it and what it does not do (yet).

## Versions

- **1.37.1**: **fix: one test failed on Windows** (`npm test`, so also the Windows build with checks). `tests/arduinosketch.test.js` opens the zip of the Arduino sketch with Python as a second opinion; on Windows Python ends its lines with CR LF and the test did not expect that, so *Python opens it, every CRC checks, and a file reads back identical* failed although the zip was fine. The test now ignores the CR, compares the file as bytes (no text encoding in the way), tries `python3`, `python` and `py -3`, and, when the machine has no Python at all, says so and skips the two Python checks instead of failing. Nothing in the app changed. How it was found: Dutch's build screen showed the single failing line; the same failure was reproduced here with a Python that prints CR LF, and the new test passes with it.
- **1.37.0**: **the Arduino box (the app's side)**. A new page, App Settings > Timing Hardware > **Arduino**, for a timing box you build yourself: an Arduino **Nano or Mega** with the SlotForge sketch, lane sensors, **pit sensors**, **start lights**, **track and lane power relays**, a buzzer and the Start and Track call buttons. On the page: **Find the box**, **Connect**, the pins with their roles and thresholds, **Calibrate**, **Test the lights**, **Power on/off**, **Beep**, **Save to the box**, a log of what the box says, and **Download the Arduino sketch** (the sketch is inside the app, as a zip with the README). **Use the virtual box** gives four imaginary cars to try everything without a board. In a race the box counts the laps (also the ones driven while the computer was away, with their own times), starts the pit stops, and the **start lights, the flags, the track power and the beeps follow the race**; the Start and Track call buttons work as the Trackmate's, and the speed-trap figure is kept on the car. The race setup has a new folder **SlotForge box (Arduino)** with four settings (what starts a pit stop, the lane power of a retired car, the beeps, the lane colours). The box cuts the track power by itself when the app has been silent for 3 seconds, and the main window no longer slows its timers when it is minimized (`backgroundThrottling: false`), so that never happens by mistake. The firmware (v1.0.3) has been checked **on a computer only**: a desktop test bench (`npm run fwtest`, needs g++) runs the real sketch against a simulated clock, ADC, serial port and buttons; it has **not been on a board yet**. Not built yet: the jump-start settings (a car crossing during the countdown is ignored) and the speed-trap widget. Behind it: `renderer/arduinobox.js` (the protocol), `arduinosim.js` (the virtual box), `arduinorace.js` (the race glue), `arduinoui.js` (the page), `arduinosketch.js` (the embedded sketch; `npm run sketch` repacks it), `zipfile.js`; `firmware/SlotForgeBox/`, `tools/` and `docs/arduino-box/` (build guide, design, README) are source only and not in the installer. Tests: `tests/arduinobox.test.js`, `arduinosim.test.js`, `arduinorace.test.js`, `arduinosketch.test.js`, the new `tests/boxscreen.test.js` (the race screen with the box) and new parts of `tests/screens.test.js` and `tests/help.test.js`. The Help has a folder *The Arduino box*.
- **1.36.0**: **the Driver stats page is a grid of your own**. On the Live Race tab, under **Driver stats**, **Customize** now works like the Leaderboard page: pick the grid with the little squares (up to **4 columns by 8 rows**), pick a cell and choose what it shows, and let a cell cover more columns and rows. Every cell has a label that can be switched off. The cells: position, lap, lap time, last lap, best, average and median lap, gap to the leader, record lap time, speed, **laps to go**, distance driven and distance to go, the **Swap signal** and **Swap timer**, the **current** and **next driver**, fuel, **tyres**, **damage**, pitstop time, penalty points, the **flag**, the **start lights**, championship points and the list of laps. The standard layout (a swap signal across the top, the position big beside the last and best lap, the gap and laps to go, the swap timer with fuel and damage, tyres and flag, the list of laps) is the **default**: a cell the race has nothing for (no driver swaps, no fuel...) is left blank and a row with nothing in it is not drawn, so a phone does not waste room. The grid you set **on the computer is the default for every device**; a phone that changes it keeps **its own grid on that phone** and has a button **Use the computer's layout again**. A device **remembers its lane** (switch it off with *Remember my lane on this device*). Behind it: the grid rules of the Leaderboard (`livegrid.js`) now come in any size (`make(columns, rows)`), the new rules are in `renderer/drivergrid.js`, and a phone can take a setting of its own out again (a `null` in `webapi.js`). Tests: `tests/drivergrid.test.js` (the rules and the phone's own settings) and new parts of `tests/live.test.js`, `tests/endurancescreen.test.js`, `tests/distancescreen.test.js`, `tests/weatherscreen.test.js` and `tests/help.test.js`. The Help has a folder *Driver stats*.

- **1.35.1**: **driver swaps by distance**, the second half of Distance Racing. In the folder *Endurance: driver swaps* of a Race Setup the swaps can now be counted in **Distance** (next to minutes and laps), in metres or in feet as App Settings say. A swap is due after so many metres (*every so often*), at fixed distances of the race, or free within the shortest and longest stint; the least and most a driver drives in the whole race are distances too, and the warning comes some metres before. *Where a swap is made* is **at the line** (the car is seen there, so the swap is due at the first crossing past the distance) or **anywhere on the track** (the distance is estimated between two crossings from the last laps, so the swap is due at the right moment). It works in an Endurance Race by time, by laps, by distance and by heats; the team, penalty and next-driver rules are the same. The **Swap Timer** shows *Swap in 12 m*. A new widget, **Swap Signal** (group Team, per lane), is big and quiet until the swap is due, then shows SWAP in red, flashing, with who comes out and who steps in (*Alex → Sam*), and stays until the swap is made, saying how late the team is; it can also show in amber when the swap is near. The audio call *swap soon* says the distance ({swapin}). The results give every driver and every stint the distance driven. Home warns when a lane has no length, and the Home card with the rules tells the swaps (it now shows them for an Endurance Race at all). Help: Endurance Race and Distance races know the swaps by distance.
- **1.35.0**: **Distance Racing**: a race can be driven for a **distance** instead of laps. The Race Setup has a new setting **The race is limited by** (a number of laps or a distance) for a race, and a *By distance* choice for the heats of a Heats race and for an Endurance Race. You set the **Distance of the race** (metres or feet), what the lanes drive (**the same distance**, **the same number of laps** or **a distance for each lane**), how a distance that is not a whole number of laps is rounded (up or to the nearest lap) and an optional longest time. Every lane drives the laps its length needs; the cars are ranked by who reached the distance first, the others by the distance driven. Home warns when the track or a lane has no length. Three new widgets in the group Distance (**Distance Driven**, **Distance To Go**, **Laps To Go**, per lane, smooth or exact), text variables for them, the summaries (Home, Race Setups, header, heat plan, results, report) and the audio calls know about distances. The session type *Race by laps* is now called *Race by laps or distance*. Help has a new folder, Distance races.

- **1.34.0**: a new Home card, **Service Due**: the slot cars that need servicing, or soon will. The cars that are due (laps since the last service have reached the service interval) come first, the car with the most laps over on top, then the cars coming up (within 10% of their interval, set in Customize), the fewest laps to go first. Each car shows *Service due* (red) or *22 laps to go* (amber) with its laps (*342 of 300 laps · 42 over*), a bar and its picture. With the bar and the picture switched off the card is compact: a dot, the name and *42 laps over* / *due now* / *22 laps to go*. Click a car and the Database opens on its card. Customize: Show (Due and coming up, or Due only), Coming up means within (5, 10, 20, 25%), Cars shown, Bars, Pictures. It works from the cars, so it shows before any race is saved; with no car in need it says all good, and with no service interval anywhere it says where to set one.
- **1.33.0**: more **statistics for a slot car**, and a **Service due** mark. The Statistics folder of a slot car (Database > Slotcars) has new figures: **Services Done** (how many times you pressed Reset when you saved; a car with a day of service from before counts 1), **Mileage Since Last Service**, **Last Driven**, **Sessions Driven**, **Wins** (first place in a race, a timed race, a heat race or an endurance race), **Deslots** (laps slower than the deslot time of their track, pit stop laps left out), **Fastest Lap Ever** (with its track) and a table **Best Lap per Track** (laps, best lap and average lap of every track). They are worked out from your laps, sessions and tracks each time the form opens, so they always match your races; a Time Token Session is not in them. Pressing Reset shows the next Services Done and a mileage since the service of 0 at once. The card of a slot car shows the same, with **Next Service** above them. A car whose laps since the last service have reached its interval has a **Service due** mark next to its name, in the grid and in the list. The Home card for the cars that need servicing comes after a mockup.
- **1.32.0**: a **Tyre Change** folder in every race. The tyre choice of 1.31.0 moved out of the Weather folder into a folder of its own, purple, after Weather (and before Endurance), on the race screen and on the Race Director page (also on a phone), and it is there in **every race**: single races, timed races, heats, practice and qualifying, with or without a weather system. A Time Token Session has none (its lanes have no cars). For each lane it shows the driver, the tyres the car is on and a **Tyre choice**: Hard, Medium, Soft, Supersoft, Intermediate or Wet. In a race with a weather system only the dry tyres can be chosen (Wet and Intermediate still come from the pit stops, and are greyed out); without weather all six can. Pick one and the car is on it at once. The tick box **Also change the tyre choice of the Slotcar in the Database** works as before. A tyre given before the car has driven a lap says so in the result (*Hard tyres, set by the Race Director before the first lap*). The Weather folder keeps the weather buttons and the tyres the cars are on, but no longer has the choice.
- **1.31.0**: the Race Director can give a car another **dry tyre** during a race. In a race with a weather system, the Weather controls (race screen and Race Director page, also on a phone) have a new column **Dry tyre**: a choice for each lane with Hard, Medium, Soft and Supersoft. Pick one and the car is on it at once, for example when a driver goes from Soft to Hard in an endurance race. A tick box under the table, **Also change the tyre choice of the Slotcar in the Database**, saves the new tyre on the slot car too (Database > Slotcars > Tyre choice); not ticked, the change is for this race only (the box is remembered while the app is open). It works in every weather and until the race is over, in single races and in heats (without the tick box the change counts for the heat that is running; the next heat starts from the Database). A car that is on Wet keeps them until its next stop; the new dry tyre is the one it goes back to. Wet and Intermediate still come from the pit stops (a car whose slot car has a Wet tyre choice and is given a dry tyre in the rain is asked to change to Wet like any other car). The result notes it (*Hard tyres, set by the Race Director after lap 14*), and a race that is resumed keeps the new tyre. The phone page asks the race window to do it, and the server only accepts a lane from 0 to 15, a dry tyre and a save that is true or false.
